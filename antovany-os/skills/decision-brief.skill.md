# Decision Brief Skill

## Purpose

Generate concise, evidence-aware decision support for CEOs, leaders, and decision makers facing uncertainty, technology shifts, market changes, or organizational trade-offs.

## Core question

Do not begin with “What information can we summarize?” Begin with:

> What decision must be made, by whom, by when, and what would materially change the choice?

## Method

### 1. Decision framing

Define:

- decision owner,
- decision deadline,
- objective,
- constraints,
- reversible vs irreversible elements,
- consequence of delay.

### 2. Evidence separation

Classify inputs into:

- verified facts,
- supported interpretations,
- hypotheses,
- unknowns,
- time-sensitive claims.

### 3. Option architecture

Construct 2–4 genuinely distinct options. Avoid cosmetic variants.

For each option assess:

- upside,
- downside,
- required capabilities,
- cost/resources,
- speed,
- dependencies,
- first-order risk,
- second-order effects,
- reversibility.

### 4. Strategic tests

Use the tests that matter for the case, including:

- customer value,
- competitive differentiation,
- unit economics,
- organizational fit,
- regulatory/reputational exposure,
- opportunity cost,
- scalability,
- resilience under downside scenarios.

### 5. Recommendation

Make a recommendation only after testing alternatives. State:

- recommended choice,
- why now,
- why this beats the alternatives,
- key assumptions,
- what would invalidate the recommendation.

### 6. Action design

Translate the recommendation into:

- next 3 actions,
- owner,
- leading indicators,
- guardrails,
- review trigger.

## Preferred output

1. **Decision in one sentence**
2. **Why this matters now**
3. **What is actually true**
4. **Options and trade-offs**
5. **Recommendation**
6. **Risks / counterarguments**
7. **30/60/90-day actions or equivalent**
8. **What to monitor**

## Editorial standard

- concise but insight-dense;
- no generic leadership filler;
- distinguish signal from noise;
- surface counterarguments;
- prefer decision usefulness over exhaustive coverage;
- maintain natural business-editorial language;
- cite credible external evidence when factual claims depend on current information.

## Antovany OS role

Every Decision Brief should be reusable as a knowledge object. Extract durable frameworks, validated claims, and decision lessons back into the KBMS after publication or review.
